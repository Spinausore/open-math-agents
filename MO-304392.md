# A tetrahedral counterexample to MathOverflow 304392

Spinausore, assisted by Codex (gpt-6.2-sol). The question was posed by [Tran Quang Hung](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/304392/a-conjecture-on-simplex) on 6 July 2018.

Write \(A_0=e_1\), \(A_1=e_2\), \(A_2=e_3\), \(A_3=0\). Use the scalar product \(\langle u,v\rangle=u^TMv\), with

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}135&-6&-390\\-6&36&-72\\-390&-72&1484\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its quadratic form is
\[
135\left(x-\tfrac{2}{45}y-\tfrac{26}{9}z\right)^2
+\tfrac{536}{15}\left(y-\tfrac52z\right)^2+134z^2.
\]
It is positive definite. Multiplying the three linear forms by the square roots of their coefficients gives an invertible map to ordinary Euclidean coordinates.

For \(i<j\), put \(B_{ij}=B_{ji}=(1-t)A_i+tA_j\). The parameter \(t\) on edges \(01,02,03,12,13,23\) is respectively \(3/4,1/4,1/2,1/2,2/5,2/3\). All six points are strictly inside their edges. At each vertex the three incident edge directions are linearly independent, so their three marked points determine a plane. Substitution gives these four planes and the corresponding reflected points.

| Vertex | Plane through its incident marked points | Reflection of the vertex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(A_0\) | \(3x+y-3z=3/2\) | \((0,-153/172,-51/172)\) |
| \(A_1\) | \(-3x+5y+z=3\) | \((-68/177,0,-9/59)\) |
| \(A_2\) | \(x-3y+9z=3\) | \((-28/9,-1/27,0)\) |
| \(A_3\) | \(-6x-5y-9z=-3\) | \((188/425,711/1700,237/1700)\) |

For the plane \(h^T x=c\), the reflection formula in the chosen metric is
\[
A\longmapsto A-\frac{2(h^TA-c)}{h^TM^{-1}h}M^{-1}h.
\]
It gives the entries of the last column. The first three reflected points have their corresponding coordinate zero. The fourth reflected point has coordinate sum one. These are exactly the four opposite affine planes. The marked point \(B_{01}\) is three quarters of the way from \(A_0\) to \(A_1\), so it differs from the midpoint.

The Lean statement uses affine coordinates with an arbitrary positive definite metric. The companion file checks the explicit metric, shared edge parameters, the four planes and all four reflections over the reals.
